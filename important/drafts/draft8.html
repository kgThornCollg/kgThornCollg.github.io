personal essay - draft 7 - (spiders)



                        Spider-Man is my favorite superhero. There's 
                    actually multiple spider-men-- Miles Morales, Gwen 
                    Stacy, etc-- but I resonate with Peter Parker's 
                    spider-man the most.

                        Although I am not as selfless as Peter, many of my choices 
                    have also been defined by responsibility. I consider myself 
                    monumentally lucky; I have virtually unlimited access to 
                    warm food, clean water, technology, a noourishing education, 
                    kind friends, the list goes on. As a beneficiary of incredible
                    circumstance, is it not my responsibility to try my best in absolutely
                    everything? I once heard "You try too hard," and I don't understand in 
                    what universe this would be suboptimal. I am not afraid of failure; 
                    I believe that as long as you keep trying you can never truly fail. 
                    I have made mistakes, slipped up, and failed. But it feels good to commit 
                    to something wholeheartedly while letting nothing keep you down, 
                    and I think more people need to understand that the only true form of failure is giving up. 
                    I believe it is my responsibility to learn, and I will never fail at learning.

                        Thus, I consider education to be one of the most important 
                    things in life. To me, Spider-Man is a symbol of perserverance. 
                    No matter what he faces, he will always try his hardest and remain steadfast in 
                    his faith in himself. In reality being alive can often feel like banging your head against 
                    a brick wall-- nothing really goes anywhere, and you're left feeling empty and confused. 
                    I think true survival is the endurance of identity, and in a world that homogenizes 
                    spirit, knowledge is the only form of defense. To have a notebook and pencil 
                    is to begin to think, feel, and enjoy in unlimited and unstoppable ways.

                        Spider-Man teaches the value of perserverance, responsibility, and learning. 
                    But the teacher itself is important too. They are found in every corner of experience 
                    and can be anything. A teacher is not simply a person beside a whiteboard. They are the impressions that life 
                    gifts us. They are the lingering feelings upon waking from a dream, a tree crying rain in the sun, or a certain arachnid themed hero. 
                    I think society is facing an existential threat in this regard-- people refusing to listen, or teachers being unable to teach. 
                    There is a very delicate balance in this world between those who teach and those who learn. 
                    I have yet to experience it fully, but I believe it to be quite possibly be the meaning of life. 
                    To this end, I will continue to learn for as long as I can, and maybe, one day,
                    teach.
