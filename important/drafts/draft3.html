personal essay - draft 3 - (duel)



One day in 8th grade, I had a cool idea for this animation I wanted to make. I had been animating for a couple months at that point, so I was an amateur at best. In that moment, a promise was made-- I would commit everything I had to this project, and finish it.


The cartoon would be fairly simple, consisting of a duel between two robots while awesome rock music played, lasting around 3 minutes. I dont know or remember why this captured my imagination. I think at the time I just thought it was cool.


One time I was animating during a storm. The turbulent air frequently knocked out the power, erasing the hours of progress I had made that day. Instead of stopping and waiting for the storm to end, I did something else. I took a breath, and started again. Power went out again. I started again. Power went out a third time. I started a third time. On and on the duel went between me and entropy. Nobody really won. Due to the repeated data loss, the net progress I made was very little. It likely wouldnt have made a difference if I had just given up and started working after the storm. But I didn't care. I had to keep going.


I think this is a trait I am proud of but simultaneously find terror-inducing. I can be driven to a degree where, over a 2 year period, I spend thousands of hours on a cheesy animation. Earlier this year, I spent 19 hours straight finishing a 7 day video-game development project. But that focus isnt healthy-- it leads to unreasonability, shortsightedness, even a little masochism.


Two years later, the project was finished. It was a turbulent process, filled with setbacks and roadblocks. I spent 6 months without a clear structure, then scrapped what was incoherently there. Then I worked on a storyboard, key-poses, animation, cleanup, coloring, shading, and finally, the finishing touches. Each part took multiple months.


I didnt recieve much of a reward upon completing the animation. I released it online to a couple thousand views (which wasn't much relative to the effort), and what felt like loose recognition from my peers. But I don't care. The process of creation is the most valuable part. Throw me back in time, and I would do it all over again. Except this time I work harder.
