personal essay - draft 7 - (spiders)



                        Spider-Man is my favorite superhero. There's 
                    actually multiple spider-men-- Miles Morales, Gwen 
                    Stacy, etc-- but I resonate with Peter Parker's 
                    spider-man the most.

                        Although I am not as selfless as Peter, many of my choices 
                    have been defined by responsibility. I consider myself 
                    monumentally lucky; I have virtually unlimited access to 
                    warm food, clean water, technology, a noourishing education, 
                    kind friends, the list goes on. As a beneficiety of incredible
                    circumstance, is it not my responsibility to try my best in absolutely
                    everything? I once heard "You try too hard," and I don't understand in 
                    what universe this would be suboptimal.

                        To me, Spider-Man is also a symbol of endurance. He will go above and 
                    beyond for his friends, and no matteer what happens to him, he always gets back up. 
                    For me, I dont see failure as failure; I believe that as long as you keep 
                    trying you can never truly fail. I have made mistakes, slipped up, and failed. 
                    But it feels good to commit to something wholeheartedly while letting nothing keep you down, 
                    and I think more people need to understand that the only true form of failure is giving up. 
                    It is my responsibility to learn, and I will never fail at learning.

                        Thus, I consider education to be one of the most important 
                    things in life. Being alive can often feel like banging your head against a brick 
                    wall-- nothing really goes anywhere and youre left feeling empty and confused. 
                    True survival is the endurance of identity, and in a woirld that homogenizes spirit,
                    knowledge is the only form of defense. I am reminded of Spider-Man swinging through 
                    New-York despereately racing to save the ones he loves. Understanding is a necessary 
                    skill one must develop-- to have a notebook and pencil is to begin to think, feel, 
                    and enjoy in unlimited ways.

                        Spider-Man taught me the value of perserverance, responsibility, and learning. 
                    But the teacher itself is important too. They are fouind in every corner of experience 
                    and take many different forms. I think society is facing an existential threat in 
                    this regard-- people refusing to listen, or teachers being unwilling or unable to teach. 
                    There is a very delicate balance in this world between thoise who teach and thsoe who learn. 
                    I have yet to experience it fully, but I believe it to be quite possible the meaning of life. 
                    To this end, I will continue to learn for as long as I can, and maybe, one day,
                    teach.
