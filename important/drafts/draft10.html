personal essay - draft 9 - (steelsmoke)



                    Hollow howls ring across the alleyways and halls of Saturn. Former watchful eyes and eager hands lay dormant, 
                    their memory flitting across the savage wind and carried into the dark sky. Deeper within,
                    numerous blazing hearts pump fuel to a forgotten goddess seething with indignity.

                    I grow in paradise. I have unlimited access to warm food, clean water, powerful technology, 
                    and a nourishing education. Thus, as a beneficiary of life's bounty, is it not my responsibility to try my absolute best
                    in everything? However, game design was never expected of me; I make precisely because I do not have to. Creating 
                    games is a means of surviving that I have no control over. It is a promise inspiring me to care for the things I create, 
                    and through that care endure adversity. As I roam above the clouds but beneath the crumbling copper streets, I feel as if 
                    I'm reclaiming some part of myself.
                    
                    As I construct my game, more and more ideas begin to fill my head. I erect poles on the cliffs of each building, and 
                    program wires to swing between them. I begin researching the mechanics of suspension and soon I discover the catenary.
                    
                    The catenary curve is a mathematical description of a hanging cable. They are separate from parabolas, although they look similar,
                    and are utilized in architecture. Thomas Jefferson first coined the word catenary, referencing the mechanical 
                    equilibrium of arches. That day I did not just render a wire. I connected gargantuan towers across the sky, and in doing so 
                    unlocked an entirely new context of the world. Whenever I look outside I see catenaries: a bracelet dangling off someone's 
                    wrist, power cables strewn around town, or, my personal favorite, the Christmas lights hanging outside my window.

                    Magic lurks in independent learning, especially when inspired by creative pursuits. Living often feels like 
                    banging your head against a brick wall-- nothing really goes anywhere, and you're left feeling empty and confused. 
                    When I look up at the clouds, I don't think about my worries. Instead, I recall the Naiver-Stokes equations and their 
                    role in fluid dynamics; I remember the countless hours I spent studying and tinkering to construct a smoke simulator;
                    and then I remember myself-- the love I have for what I learn and create. And all my existential 
                    doubts vanish, like smoke rising to the sky.

                    There is a more important phenomenon which takes place while I learn. When I wanted to 
                    optimize my catenaries by replacing them with bezier curves, the knowledge of De Castelijau's algorithm did not wander 
                    into my brain. I was taught. By teaching we inspire mutual care,
                    connection, and the encouragement to continue learning and to teach others. When I teach my characters to jump a certain 
                    height or talk with certain sounds, my characters also teach me about two-dimensional kinematics and pitch variance; A teacher can be a person 
                    beside a whiteboard, but also the lingering sadness upon waking from a dream, fresh rain dripping from trees bathing in the sun,
                    or a chain draped between two steel posts. In this world, there exists a very delicate balance between those who teach and those who 
                    learn. I have yet to experience this balance fully, but I believe it to be quite possibly the meaning of life. To this end, I will continue
                    to learn for as long as I can, and maybe one day, 
                    teach.
