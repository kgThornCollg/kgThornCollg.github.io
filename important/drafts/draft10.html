personal essay - draft 8 - (spiders)



                        Spider-Man is my favorite superhero. There's 
                    actually multiple Spider-Men-- Miles Morales, Gwen 
                    Stacy, etc-- but I resonate with Peter Parker's 
                    spider-man the most.

                        Although I am not as selfless as Peter, many of my choices 
                    have also been defined by responsibility. I consider myself 
                    monumentally lucky; I have virtually unlimited access to 
                    warm food, clean water, technology, a nourishing education, 
                    kind friends, the list goes on. As a beneficiary of incredible
                    circumstance, is it not my responsibility to try my best in absolutely
                    everything? I once heard "You try too hard," and I don't understand in 
                    what universe this would be suboptimal. Because as long as you keep trying 
                    you can never truly fail. It feels good to commit to something wholeheartedly while letting 
                    nothing keep you down, and I think more people need to understand 
                    that the only true form of failure is giving up. 
                    
                        Something else I admire about Spider-man is his tenacity. We live in a compromised world,
                    and we are forced to make compromises to continue existing. Much like we lack control over our own lives,
                    Spider-Man gets hit with things he doesn't recognize. What seperates him from us and elevates him 
                    into myth, however, is that he doesn't sacrifice his ideals. As someone who feels 
                    like they lack control over the things that happen to them, the idea that I could 
                    face harrowing events and still remain myself is everything to me. Spider-Man
                    inspires me to embrace myself, face adversity, and continue learning.

                        Spider-Man is an intelligent individual, but that doesn't really matter to me.
                    Smartness isnt important-- understanding is. Learning is a form of self-assurance that 
                    we live in a sane world and everything makes sense, even if that isnt really true. 
                    Moreover, there is something much more magical and inexplicable about learning. 
                    Being alive can often feel like banging your head against a brick wall; nothing 
                    really goes anywhere, and you're left feeling empty and confused. Like I said,
                    we make compromises in a compromised world. Thus I think true survival is the 
                    endurance of identity, and in a world that homogenizes spirit, knowledge is the 
                    only form of defense. To have a notebook and pencil is to begin to think, feel, 
                    and enjoy in unlimited and unstoppable ways.

                        But learning is only one piece of the puzzle. We live in context of another,
                    and so to look at things completely individualistically would be unfair. 
                    Teaching is just as important as learning, and by teaching we inspire something much
                    greater than learning in each other. So what is inspired? Love for one another,
                    the formation of community, the repealment of fear, and the encouragement to continue learning 
                    and to teach others. In this world, there exists a very delicate balance between those who teach and those who learn. 
                    A teacher is not simply a person beside a whiteboard. They are found in every corner of experience 
                    and can be anything. They are the impressions that life gifts us: the lingering feelings upon waking from a dream, 
                    a tree crying rain in the sun, or a certain arachnid themed hero. 
                    I think society is facing an existential threat in this regard-- people refusing to listen, or teachers being unable to teach. 
                    I have yet to experience this balance fully, but I believe it to be quite possibly be the meaning of life. 
                    To this end, I will continue to learn for as long as I can, and maybe, one day,
                    teach.
